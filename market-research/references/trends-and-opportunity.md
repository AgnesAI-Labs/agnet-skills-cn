# Industry Trends and Opportunity Assessment

Use this reference for industry structure, market movement, regulatory or technology shifts, entry decisions, whitespace, or prioritizing opportunities.

## Map Relevant Market Forces

Define the industry's value chain and major customer, supplier, distribution, and competitor groups when those relationships matter. Identify structural features such as concentration/fragmentation, switching costs, network effects, barriers to entry, substitutes, and the role of regulation. Use frameworks such as PESTLE or Five Forces as optional lenses, not mandatory report sections.

For each trend or market force, record what changed, when, which geography/segment it affects, the supporting evidence, likely direction and timeframe, and the main uncertainty. Distinguish observed changes from forecasts and speculative scenarios.

## Assess Opportunities

For each candidate opportunity, describe:

- The customer segment and unmet need or job.
- Evidence of demand and current alternatives.
- Why existing offers do not fully address it, if evidence supports that claim.
- Reachability, distribution, capability, regulatory, and timing constraints.
- The strongest risk or disconfirming evidence.
- A low-cost next validation step and the signal that would change the decision.

Rank opportunities only when the criteria and evidence are explicit. Separate a sourced market signal from an inferred opportunity. A recommendation such as GO, CONDITIONAL, PIVOT, or NO-GO is optional and must follow from the user's decision criteria and evidence, not from an arbitrary score.
