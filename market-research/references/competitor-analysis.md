# Competitor and Alternative Analysis

Use this reference when the user wants to discover competitors, understand a competitive landscape, compare products or positioning, or find differentiation opportunities.

## Define the Competitive Frame

Start from the customer, job, geography, and time horizon. Identify the actual choice a customer makes, including:

- **Direct competitors:** serve a similar customer and solve the same job with a comparable offer.
- **Indirect competitors:** solve the same job through a different product, service, or delivery model.
- **Substitutes and status quo:** internal tools, agencies, spreadsheets, manual processes, or doing nothing.
- **Emerging or adjacent entrants:** include only when there is evidence of a plausible move into the defined market.

Do not equate every company using similar keywords with a competitor. If the category or comparison set is ambiguous and would materially affect the answer, show a short candidate list and ask the user to confirm or correct it before deep profiling.

## Discover and Profile

Use the user's known names/URLs as seeds, then search for additional candidates if discovery is requested. Prefer primary evidence from company/product pages, pricing pages, product documentation, filings, and official announcements. Customer reviews, job posts, public discussions, and third-party estimates are useful signals, but label their source type and limits.

Compare entities using the same applicable dimensions, such as:

- Target customer, use case, and market/geography.
- Product or service, core capabilities, limitations, and integrations.
- Positioning and value promise, distinguishing marketing claims from observed product facts.
- Pricing model and public price points; mark unavailable or quote-based pricing.
- Distribution, sales model, partnerships, and public traction signals.
- Strengths, weaknesses, customer feedback, and recent verified moves.

Do not invent market share, revenue, customer counts, traction, or pricing. If evidence is unavailable, mark it unknown rather than scoring it as zero.

## Synthesize

Use a comparison matrix for a multi-entity request. Add a positioning map, SWOT, or Five Forces only when it helps answer the user's decision and there is enough evidence to support it. Identify differentiated strengths, meaningful gaps, substitutes, and which customer segments those gaps matter to. A gap in a feature checklist is not automatically a market opportunity; connect it to customer evidence or label it as a hypothesis.

For each consequential comparison, include supporting citations and distinguish observed facts, customer signal, and strategic inference. State the competitor-selection scope so a short list is not mistaken for an exhaustive census.
