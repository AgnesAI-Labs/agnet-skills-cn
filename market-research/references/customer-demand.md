# Customer Segments and Demand Signals

Use this reference when the user asks who may buy, what customers need, how they decide, whether demand is credible, or what customer research to conduct.

## Select the Evidence Mode

Keep these evidence modes separate:

1. **Analyze supplied material:** interview notes/transcripts, survey results, reviews, support tickets, CRM notes, or usage data. State the source, sample, date range, and limitations.
2. **Mine public signals:** reviews, forums, communities, public posts, search-interest proxies, and public case studies. Treat the collected material as a non-representative sample unless a valid sampling design supports stronger claims.
3. **Design primary research:** propose interview questions, a survey, or a sampling plan. Design work is not evidence that the research was conducted.
4. **Conduct primary research:** only when appropriate tools, participants, authorization, and actual response data are available. Do not contact people or submit forms without explicit user authorization.

## Analyze Segments and Needs

Segment by needs, behavior, job, urgency, willingness-to-pay evidence, organization type/size, geography, or other factors relevant to the decision. Demographics alone may not explain buying behavior. Describe each segment's job or problem, current workaround, trigger, purchase process, decision criteria, and barriers when evidence exists.

Look for evidence of behavior as well as stated preferences: repeated workarounds, active search, budget ownership, switching, trial, pre-order, or payment. Do not treat compliments, likes, generic survey agreement, or a few online complaints as proof of demand or willingness to pay.

## Communicate Limits

- Report the sample/source composition and collection period when known.
- Separate direct participant statements from analyst interpretation.
- Avoid claims of representativeness without an appropriate sample and method.
- Do not construct a buyer persona from unsupported demographic assumptions.
- Identify the unanswered question that most affects the decision and suggest a proportionate next research step.
