# Market Sizing and Forecasting

Use this reference when the user asks how large a market or reachable opportunity is, how it is changing, or how to estimate a future value. Read only when the request needs sizing or forecasting.

## Define the Number First

Before searching, define:

- What is counted: revenue, spending, units, customers, valuation, or addressable opportunity.
- Which products, services, customer groups, and parts of the value chain are included or excluded.
- Geography, base year, currency, and whether values are actuals, estimates, or forecasts.
- Whether the user needs the whole category (TAM), the portion the offer can serve (SAM), or a realistic obtainable portion over a specified time (SOM).

Do not assume a fixed percentage of TAM for SAM or SOM. Derive each boundary from customer eligibility, geography, product fit, access, competition, capacity, and time horizon.

## Estimate with Suitable Methods

Use one method for a quick directional scan when evidence is limited. For a consequential sizing decision, use two independent approaches when possible:

- **Top-down:** start with a credible broader market or official dataset, then show each inclusion/exclusion filter and the source or assumption behind it.
- **Bottom-up:** estimate eligible customers or units multiplied by a grounded price, usage, or purchase frequency, with adoption and reach assumptions stated separately.

Present the methods side by side. Explain their divergence; do not choose the larger number to make the opportunity look attractive. If the data cannot support two independent estimates, say so and lower confidence.

## Check Arithmetic and Comparability

- Preserve the source's reported value and unit. Show any currency or unit conversion and recheck the arithmetic.
- Check the stated CAGR against the start value, end value, and number of periods. Do not silently replace a source's rate with a recalculated one.
- Keep estimates with different market definitions, base years, geographies, or forecast periods separate.
- Do not merge software revenue with total industry spending that includes hardware, infrastructure, or services unless the definitions genuinely match.
- Label third-party estimates and forecasts as such. A precise number is not necessarily a reliable number.

## Recommended Output

For each estimate, report the metric, value and unit, scope/definition, geography, year or period, method, key assumptions, source(s), and confidence. Include a range when inputs are uncertain. State what new evidence would most reduce uncertainty.
